# 03 · Competitive Landscape

Competitors are described as **archetypes** rather than by name. This keeps the analysis portable and avoids revealing the specific niche.

## Competitor Archetypes

| Archetype | Description | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| A · Mass-market brands | National CPG brands in grocery | Distribution, price, awareness | Commodity quality, little provenance story |
| B · Premium DTC brands | Venture-backed online-first brands | Brand, content, subscriptions | High CAC, broad but shallow assortment |
| C · Specialty importers | Small importers selling to ethnic / specialty grocers | Authentic sourcing, low cost | Weak branding, limited online presence |
| D · Marketplace private label | Low-cost sellers on large marketplaces | Price, convenience | Inconsistent quality, low trust |

## Positioning Map (conceptual)

        A · Mass market    │

**White-space hypothesis:** archetype C's authentic sourcing paired with archetype B's brand and digital experience, at a price point just below the premium DTC leaders.

## Porter's Five Forces

| Force | Intensity | Rationale |
|-------|:---------:|-----------|
| Threat of new entrants | **High** | Low capital needs and easy marketplace access |
| Supplier power | **Medium** | Sourcing is fragmented, but top-quality supply is limited |
| Buyer power | **Medium–High** | Many substitutes and low switching costs |
| Threat of substitutes | **Medium** | Mass-market alternatives are "good enough" for many buyers |
| Competitive rivalry | **Medium–High** | Growing category with active premium entrants |

**Implication:** defensibility has to come from **supply relationships, quality consistency and brand / community**, not from scale or price.
